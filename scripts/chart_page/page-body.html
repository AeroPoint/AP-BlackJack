Where the money leaks

A basic-strategy chart tells you what to do and nothing about what it costs to be wrong. Standing on 20 instead of hitting is a catastrophe; standing on 16 against a ten instead of hitting is worth about six thousandths of a bet. Both are one coloured square.

Set the rules to the table in front of you, then colour the chart by money instead of by play. There are two different money questions and they have two different answers, so they get two separate views: cost if wrong is what one mistake on that square costs you, and where it leaks is where your money actually goes once you account for how often the hand turns up and how likely anyone is to misplay it. Standing on 20 is the most expensive mistake on the chart and also leaks nothing, because nobody makes it. Tap any square for both numbers in plain English.

Study order

Ranked by what a typical learner actually loses here per hundred rounds — the EV margin, times how often the hand arises, times how likely they are to misplay it. Numbered because it is a sequence: this is the order worth drilling, and it is not the order any printed chart teaches.

    Closest calls

    Squares where the two best plays are within half a percent of a bet. Learn them, but do not agonise: these are the ones that genuinely barely matter.

      Every number on this page was computed by the solver in this project, not copied from a table. Each of the 480 rule combinations above was solved ahead of time and shipped with the page, which is why the chart changes the instant you change a rule rather than asking a server. Expected values are exact and composition-dependent: the cards you hold are cards the dealer cannot draw, so (10,6) against a ten is a different problem from (9,7) against a ten. Where the two disagree with the printed row, the square lists the exception.

      The miss rate behind the study order is a model, not a measurement: half at a true coin flip, falling away quickly as the right answer becomes obviously right. Nobody hits a twenty. Once the trainer has real session data it gets replaced by your own measured rate, and the ranking becomes personal rather than generic.
